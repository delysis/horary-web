# Reviewing Horary with Eileen

Before changing the workflow, examine the [living process map](LLM_PROCESS.md): three Mermaid diagrams trace every model/tool stage, place and time selection, and where interpretation appears. The task table distinguishes classification, horary reasoning and deterministic calculation. Exact model instructions, generated schemas and synthetic prompt examples are linked alongside the diagram. The reference is checked against the application source by a normal Rust test. The proposed place/time cases are review targets, not claims that the model has passed them.

Open the native Horary app and tell it what you want to know. Use the microphone or type. There are no settings to configure and no chart form to complete. The first conversation prepares the local model; an empty machine downloads roughly 7.15 GB into the shared Hugging Face cache, without Python, a CLI, an account, or a duplicate app copy.

The app normally obtains the device’s present location and clock context when the first question is sent. Allow the operating system’s location request if you want that default; otherwise tell it the place. A place supplied in conversation takes priority. The app should ask natural questions when it needs context. The chart and the reading appear on a scroll as the conversation develops. Ask “Why did you choose that house?”, “What does the book say?”, or “What would change if this belonged to my sister?” Ask to revisit an earlier reading; the version being left is preserved. Earlier user passages can be edited to propose corrections.

Hold Space anywhere outside editable text, or hold the speech invitation on the page. While writing, use Option–Space. Release when finished. Installed on-device dictation turns speech into words when available; otherwise the local reader hears your recording directly. Direct understanding appears as “From your spoken words,” a summary you can correct. macOS may ask once for microphone and Speech permission. Spoken replies use its installed voice. Text remains available throughout. Escape interrupts a reply or pauses model acquisition without discarding already completed work.

Please keep your own notes about what feels right, what misrepresents your process, what it assumes, and where it asks an unnecessary question. The most useful examples include the question, the relevant response, and your preferred reasoning. You do not need to fill in a bug-report form.

Useful review cases: a lost object (your own versus someone else's); an ordinary job question; an ambiguous city; a historical chart; correcting ownership after a reading has started; asking for the exact testimony behind an answer. An answer that sounds convincing still needs your judgment. A valid evidence reference does not make an interpretation correct.

The app saves this conversation and earlier chart revisions locally. The legacy review notes are preserved. This development build is not a signed public release, and native voice and conversational quality need direct acceptance. See [the conversation design](CONVERSATION.md) and [verification record](VERIFICATION.md).

Touch a planet for its calculated testimony. Open “How this follows” under a passage to compare the facts, chosen roles, book rule and interpretation. “Notes from this reading” shows progress; the local journal records timings for diagnosis. No issue form is needed. The [October 4 iteration notes](ITERATION_2026-10-04.md) explain the changes and remaining boundaries.
