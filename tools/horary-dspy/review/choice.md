I am relaying Sian’s own question: I can keep my current home or move to the offered flat. Which gives me better daily comfort?

### Worked answer

**Status:** answer

Sian, I would lean toward keeping your current home for daily comfort. The offered flat has some positive indications, but it does not show a convincing overall improvement. This is a qualified preference for staying, not a claim that your present home is especially comfortable.

### Reasoning

Because this is Sian’s own question relayed by someone else, Sian receives the radical 1st house. Aquarius on its cusp gives Saturn; the Moon also signifies her, especially her emotional comfort. This is a stay-or-change comparison: the 1st and Saturn show things as they are; the 7th and its ruler, the Sun from Leo, show life after moving. The Sun’s physical placement is in the 8th, distinct from its role as Lord 7. There is no shared-ruler or Moon collision.

Neither alternative is strong. Saturn is in fall, retrograde and within the severe solar-opposition region, although its 2nd-house position has neutral capacity. Keeping the current home is not itself a return that would clearly excuse retrogradation. The Sun is also in fall and physically in the weak 8th. Its direct motion does not cancel these problems. Nothing in the accepted circumstances makes either fall merely a fitting description of a comfortable home.

The principal argument for moving is Jupiter almost exactly on the 7th cusp: it has angular power and some essential dignity by term. This is real support for the offered flat, but Jupiter is not strongly dignified merely because it is naturally benefic. Its reception also complicates that promise: Jupiter strongly favours the Sun, representing the changed situation, while being in Saturn’s detriment. The Moon is in Jupiter’s detriment too. Thus the apparent benefit is poorly aligned with Sian’s significators.

Saturn exalts the Sun and also receives it by triplicity and face: the changed situation looks attractive to Sian, potentially excessively so. The Sun independently exalts Saturn, with triplicity and face as well: there is reciprocal positive reception. That supports the move, but both planets’ serious debilities limit how much practical benefit this mutual regard can deliver.

The Moon is angular but peregrine, waning and low in light. The supplied calculation finds it void before leaving Virgo, favouring continuation of the present situation without deciding comfort by itself. Its first listed future contact is with peregrine Mars only after ingress; this supplies no clear move toward a strongly dignified benefic. Certified separating-contact evidence is unavailable, so the full benefic-to-malefic comparison cannot be completed.

Overall, the support for moving does not convincingly outweigh its weaknesses. Under this branch’s “go if better; otherwise stay” rule, staying narrowly wins. No aspect is required to judge comparative comfort, and no moving date is established. I would not relocate Saturn hypothetically to the 7th cusp: the source expressly forbids that shortcut for this comparison.

## dspy-choice-first-020 (test)

### Question

I live in Springfield, our nearby Virginia town, and can stay or move to the available Norfolk flat. I mean home as where I live now. Would moving suit me better?

### Worked answer

**Status:** answer

I would favour staying in Springfield. The chart does not show the Norfolk move clearly suiting you better, although staying is not shown as especially comfortable either. There is attraction to the change and some support for it, but the proposed situation looks too compromised to promise a worthwhile improvement.

### Reasoning

This is a stay-or-change question about your own circumstances, not a foreign-country or ancestral-home question. Aquarius on the 1st cusp gives Saturn for you and the present situation; Leo on the 7th gives the Sun for circumstances after moving. The Moon remains your emotional cosignificator. These roles are distinct from their physical placements: Saturn is in the 2nd, the Sun in the 8th, and the Moon in the 7th. No shared-ruler substitution is needed. Your clarification about “home” does not make nearby Norfolk a 9th-house destination; nor should Saturn be artificially placed inside the 7th cusp.

Both principal rulers are in fall. Saturn’s fall, retrogradation and proximity to solar opposition make the present situation troubled. There is no declared return or retracing circumstance that clearly excuses the retrogradation. Nevertheless, Saturn has neutral house capacity, while the Sun, representing the changed situation, has weak capacity in the 8th as well as fall. Moving therefore offers no clear essential improvement and has a disadvantage in capacity. Saturn’s solar affliction is substantial contrary evidence against portraying staying as comfortable.

The receptions provide the strongest qualification. Saturn exalts the Sun and also regards it by triplicity and face: you are attracted to the change, potentially expecting too much of it. The Sun reciprocates through exaltation, triplicity and face toward Saturn, suggesting compatibility or welcome in the changed circumstances. But mutual reception does not remove either planet’s fall, and their compromised condition limits how much practical benefit it can deliver.

Jupiter near the 7th cusp is another favourable-looking testimony for moving. It has angular capacity and minor essential dignity by term, rather than major dignity. Its reception is also divided: Jupiter favours the Sun by domicile and triplicity but is in Saturn’s detriment. Thus its presence supports the proposed situation more readily than it supports you; it is not an unqualified benefic promising that Norfolk will suit you.

The Moon is angular and clear of the Sun, but peregrine and waning. Its strong regard for Mercury is not reciprocated: Mercury is in the Moon’s fall. Because Mercury’s property role does not securely distinguish these two flats, this cannot independently select Norfolk or Springfield.

The calculated Moon is void before leaving Virgo. This supports continuation or lack of impetus, rather than proving moving impossible. Its first listed contact is a conditional sextile to peregrine Mars after ingress, not an immediate encounter with a strongly dignified benefic. Later lunar contacts should not be assembled into a promised moving story. No verified separating-contact sequence supplies the chapter’s alternative “leaving bad, approaching good” testimony.

On balance, the attraction and partial support for moving do not establish a superior outcome. Staying is the default when change is not demonstrably better. This is a suitability judgment: no perfected event aspect is required, and no moving date is established.
